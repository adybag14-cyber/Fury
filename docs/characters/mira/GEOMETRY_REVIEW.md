# Mira Vale geometry and deformation review

Review date: 2026-10-06. This review exercises the actual `NpcCivA` / `Commuter`
profile generated by `make_character_profile`; it does not substitute the legacy
humanoid, a renderer proxy, or hand-written sample triangles.

## Regression coverage

`tests/character_profile_tests.cpp` checks both near and far models at 1.35 m,
1.75 m (Mira's actual roster height), and 2.10 m:

- Every index is in range; every face has three distinct indices, positive area,
  and winding consistent with its vertex normals
- Positions, unit normals, tint, opacity, and two-bone influences are finite and
  valid; all 17 rig joints influence actual vertices
- UVs remain inside the content area of one of the ten original atlas regions,
  and no triangle crosses a material-region boundary
- Both soles touch the exact ground plane at rest; the head-to-ground height and
  body depth/width remain plausible, with positive signed shell volume
- Both LODs retain the same rig, seed, appearance, shared PBR atlas, and individual
  switch distance; the reduced mesh removes more than half the geometry
- Repeated construction is bit-deterministic
- 1,188 actual sampled and skinned poses cover idle, walk, run, talk with
  attention, crouch, and crouch-walk: 33 frames per state, three heights, two LODs
- Every sampled vertex stays finite with a unit normal; every sampled triangle
  retains positive area, actual shoe vertices stay above the ground, and adjacent
  frames remain bounded
- Skinning retains vertex/index storage, index order, mesh identity, renderer
  handles, tint, UVs, and opacity; applying an identical pose does not dirty it
  twice; the bind mesh remains unchanged
- 192 fallback cases compare every geometry, rig, weight, appearance, and identity
  field bit for bit with `make_character_model`, covering the other current
  roster identities, dynamic guard/enforcer spawns, player, ghost, unknown and
  case-mismatched names, embedded NULs, and `NpcCivA` used with other roles
- Invalid heights, roles, and LODs fail explicitly for both individual and
  generic requests

Float bit comparisons intentionally ignore C++ structure padding and runtime
mesh/GPU identity counters: those are not authored geometry or identity data.
The signed volume check sums overlapping authored shells; it is not a claim
that clothing, eyes, and the body form a single watertight boolean union.

## CPU geometry budget

The recommended asset caps are 40,000 near triangles and 10,000 far triangles,
with a greater-than-50% reduction in both vertices and triangles at the far LOD.
These are asset-local ceilings, not a frame-rate promise or a world-wide NPC
budget. Runtime cost also includes skinning, ray acceleration updates, shadow
rays, resolution, and all other visible geometry. The legacy generic character's
2,500-triangle ceiling does not apply to this dedicated close-range asset.

The first draft measured 59,512 near and 16,798 far triangles (35,110 / 11,098
vertices). Although its 71.8% triangle reduction was meaningful, both LODs
exceeded the recommended individual budget. The reviewed revision uses 39,662
near and 9,344 far triangles (24,417 / 6,699 vertices): a 76.4% reduction in
triangles and 72.6% reduction in vertices when switching to far.

## Defects identified during review

1. Fractional powers of a float sine at curve endpoints produced NaNs in eyelids
   and lips. Clamping the sine to its intended nonnegative domain removed them
2. Collapsing every hair-crown point to the same position produced degenerate
   faces. A finite, shaped crown radius removed that collapse
3. The engine's general `normalize` zero-vector threshold discarded valid tiny
   cap normals on nails, eye details, and the mouth seam. At 1.75 m, the corrected
   endpoint geometry still had 664 near and 186 far zero normals. The authored
   sculpt's scale-safe normal normalization fixes these without weaker tests
4. Far hair tubes twisted at an abrupt tangent-reference change. Nine genuine
   winding/normal disagreements remained after excluding zero-normal mouth caps;
   continuous transported frames fix these curves
5. Reducing the thin shoulder bridge to six sides produced four far-only
   winding/normal disagreements. Retaining eight sides in that small component
   removed all four, costing only 20 extra far triangles

The initial deformation-only diagnostic passed all 1,188 poses once the NaNs and
crown collapse were repaired. It temporarily omitted the independently known
normal and static-geometry failures to expose any additional deformation issues.
That diagnostic is not the complete regression pass: the repository test keeps
all checks, including normals, winding, and budgets, mandatory.

Verification performed:

- Independent C++17 `-O2 -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic` build of the actual profile and
  repository regression, linked against the current Fury engine library: pass
- All six height/LOD static geometry checks, 192 bit-exact fallback cases and
  1,188 sampled poses: pass
- AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer, with the profile, generic
  model, animator and math sources instrumented: pass, no reported errors
- LeakSanitizer: unavailable in this executor because its ptrace wrapper is
  unsupported; the successful ASan/UBSan run uses `ASAN_OPTIONS=detect_leaks=0`
- Whitespace/error check for the test and this review: pass

The existing engine library supplies material/texture code in the isolated
sanitizer run; this is not an assertion that every renderer and texture routine
was rebuilt with instrumentation. The shared build was not modified for these
independent runs. The registered integrated target is
`fury_character_profile_tests`, with CTest name `character_profile`:

```sh
cmake --build build --target fury_character_profile_tests -j2
ctest --test-dir build -R '^character_profile$' --output-on-failure
```

No visual-quality parity claim follows from passing numerical geometry checks;
face, silhouette, materials, clothing construction, and motion still require the
separate real-frame review and captures.
